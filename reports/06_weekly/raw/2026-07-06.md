**Event 1｜食 — Taiwan's Soybean-Oil Carcinogen Scandal**

## **\[EN\] When 1,300 Tonnes of Cooking Oil Exposes a Broken Trust Chain**

### **Introduction**

In the last days of June 2026, Taiwan's food world was shaken by a familiar ghost. ChungLien Oil & Fat (中聯油脂) confirmed that roughly 1,300 tonnes of soybean salad oil contained benzo\[a\]pyrene — a Group 1 carcinogen — at 8.1 micrograms per kilogram, about four times the legal ceiling of 2 μg/kg. The tainted oil, produced in early April and shipped to three major refiners (FuMou, FuShou, Taishan), had already dissolved into the bloodstream of the island's food economy: at least 300 downstream businesses and 18 product lines across 30 batch numbers, reaching household brands such as Louisa Coffee, Grand Formosa hotels, Wowprime, Laurel and Lianhua Foods. By 3 July the Food and Drug Administration had ordered recalls under threat of fines from NT\$30,000 to NT\$3 million. What makes this more than a routine recall is *how* the problem surfaced: not a regulator's inspection, but a downstream customer (Namchow) voluntarily re-testing and noticing readings that were "within limits but abnormally high."

### **A Social-Innovation Reading**

Seen through a social-innovation lens, this scandal is less a story about one bad batch and more a diagnosis of a **trust architecture** that still runs on paper certificates and single-point testing. The original raw material "passed" inspection in April; the danger appeared later, invisibly, because the system tests at the gate and then assumes safety downstream. Social innovation asks a different question: how do we design food systems where *trust is continuously verifiable rather than periodically certified*?  
Three innovation frontiers stand out. First, **distributed traceability as public infrastructure.** The reason 300 businesses were caught flat-footed is that traceability data lived in private, siloed ERP systems. A shared, tamper-evident traceability ledger — whether blockchain-based or a simpler open batch-registry — would let any downstream buyer or citizen trace a batch number to its origin in seconds, turning a two-week detective hunt into a one-click query. Pilots in the EU and South Korea show this is technically mature; the barrier is governance, not code.  
Second, **the citizen and customer as distributed sensor.** The hero of this episode was not the state but Namchow's own re-testing. This is a template: what if "voluntary re-verification" were rewarded and pooled? Imagine a co-operative testing consortium where mid-sized food firms share the cost of independent labs and publish anonymised results to a common dashboard. Risk detection becomes a networked commons rather than each firm's private gamble.  
Third, **open-data recall as a design default.** The FDA's recall list circulated as PDFs and news tables — hard to search, harder to act on. A machine-readable, API-first recall feed would let restaurant POS systems, delivery apps and even home-kitchen apps flag affected products automatically. The social innovation is not the technology but the decision to treat public-health information as a real-time public utility.

### **Actionable Takeaways**

> * **For social entrepreneurs:** Build a lightweight, opt-in batch-traceability registry for small and mid-sized food producers — the ones too small for enterprise blockchain but most exposed to shared-ingredient risk. Revenue can come from insurance partners who price lower premiums for transparent supply chains.  
> * **For regulators:** Mandate machine-readable recall APIs and reward voluntary re-testing with reduced inspection burden — turning compliance into collaboration.  
> * **For citizens and communities:** Support consumer co-ops and neighbourhood "food councils" that pool testing and publish local results, converting anxiety into collective capability.  
> * **The mindset shift:** Move from "prove it was safe once" to "make safety continuously visible." Trust that can be checked is the real innovation.

## **\[繁中\] 當 1,300 公噸沙拉油，照見一條斷裂的信任鏈**

### **引言**

2026 年 6 月底，台灣的餐桌被一個熟悉的幽靈驚動。中聯油脂證實，約 1,300 公噸大豆沙拉油驗出一級致癌物「苯駢芘」，含量達每公斤 8.1 微克，超過法規上限每公斤 2 微克約四倍。這批四月初生產的問題油，已出貨給福懋、福壽、泰山三大廠，並滲入全台食品經濟的血管：截至 7 月 3 日，波及至少 300 家業者、18 個品項、30 個批號，觸及路易莎、晶華酒店、王品、桂冠、聯華食品等知名品牌。食藥署要求限期下架，未完成者可處新台幣 3 萬至 300 萬元罰鍰。然而真正值得深思的，是問題「被發現的方式」——不是來自主管機關的稽查，而是下游客戶南僑集團自行複驗，察覺數值「雖在標準內卻異常偏高」而主動追查。

### **社會創新角度分析**

從社會創新的視角看，這起事件與其說是「一批壞油」的故事，不如說是一份對「信任架構」的診斷書——一套仍靠紙本證明與單點檢驗運作的舊系統。原料四月「通過」了檢驗，危害卻在其後無聲浮現，因為系統只在入口把關，然後假設下游一路安全。社會創新提出的是另一個問題：我們能否設計出「信任可被持續驗證，而非定期認證」的食品系統？  
三個創新前沿浮現。第一，**把分散式溯源變成公共基礎設施。**300 家業者措手不及，正因溯源資料被鎖在各自封閉的 ERP 系統裡。一個共享、防竄改的溯源帳本——無論是區塊鏈或更簡單的開放批號登錄制——能讓任何下游買家或公民，在數秒內從批號回溯到源頭，把原本兩週的「偵探式追查」變成一次點擊的查詢。歐盟與韓國的試點顯示技術已然成熟，真正的門檻是治理，不是程式碼。  
第二，**讓公民與客戶成為分散式感測器。**本次事件的英雄不是國家，而是南僑的自主複驗。這是一個可複製的範本：若「自願再驗證」能被獎勵並彙整呢？想像一個檢驗合作社，讓中型食品廠共同分攤獨立實驗室的成本，並把去識別化的結果發布到共同儀表板——風險偵測於是成為一種網絡化的公共財，而非各家私下的豪賭。  
第三，**把開放資料式的回收召回設為預設值。**食藥署的下架清單以 PDF 與新聞表格流傳，難以搜尋、更難行動。一個機器可讀、以 API 為先的召回資料流，能讓餐廳 POS、外送 App 甚至家用食譜 App 自動標記受影響產品。這裡的社會創新不在技術本身，而在於「把公共衛生資訊視為即時公共設施」的這個決定。

### **可行的啟示與行動建議**

> * **對社會創業者：**為中小型食品業者打造輕量、可自選加入的批號溯源登錄平台——他們規模不足以導入企業級區塊鏈，卻最暴露於「共用原料」的連坐風險。可與保險業合作，為透明供應鏈提供較低保費作為收入來源。  
> * **對監管者：**強制推行機器可讀的召回 API，並以「減少稽查負擔」獎勵自願複驗，讓合規轉化為協作。  
> * **對公民與社區：**支持消費合作社與社區「食安理事會」，共同分攤檢驗、公布在地結果，把焦慮轉化為集體能力。  
> * **心態轉變：**從「證明它曾經安全」走向「讓安全被持續看見」。能被查核的信任，才是真正的創新。

**Event 2｜住・育 — Taiwan's Family-Support Demographic Strategy**

## **\[EN\] Designing an Economy That Makes Room for the Next Generation**

### **Introduction**

This week the operational details of Taiwan's "New Population Strategy — Family Support Chapter" dominated lifestyle conversation, as the housing pillar ("marriage-and-childrearing homes," 婚育宅／育嬰宅) moved toward implementation. Announced as an 18-measure package spanning birth, childcare, education, friendly workplaces and housing relief, its headline items are striking: a universal NT\$5,000 monthly "growth allowance" for every child aged 0–18; parental leave extended in coverage from age 3 to age 6 and doubled from 30 to 60 days; tiered childcare subsidies of up to NT\$15,000 a month; and housing measures that cut property and land-value taxes for young families and let households with children under six secure stable 12-year tenancy. Facing one of the world's lowest fertility rates, Taiwan is effectively asking whether a society can *design its way* out of demographic decline — a question that is, at heart, about everyday life: whether a young couple can afford a home and dares to raise a child in it.

### **A Social-Innovation Reading**

Fertility policy is often framed as a subsidy auction — pay parents more and hope for more babies. The international evidence is humbling: cash alone rarely moves birth rates durably. What makes this package interesting to a social-innovation observer is where it edges beyond cash toward **redesigning the infrastructure of care.**  
The first innovation is treating **housing and childrearing as a single system rather than two policy silos.** By coupling tax relief with 12-year tenancy security for families with young children, the policy attacks the real fear behind low fertility in East Asia — not the cost of a stroller, but the impossibility of stable shelter. Social innovation has long argued that *housing is the substrate of every other life decision*; bundling secure tenure with childrearing is a structural, not cosmetic, move.  
The second is the design of the **growth allowance itself.** Splitting the 6–18 payment so that half is freely usable and half is locked into a child's dedicated account is a quiet piece of behavioural design — it builds an asset-formation habit and hands each young person a small stake in the future. This echoes "child development account" experiments worldwide, which show that even modest asset-holding shifts educational aspirations. The innovation is in the mechanism, not the money.  
But the sharper social-innovation critique is that **state programmes cannot manufacture the thing fertility actually depends on: a culture and community that makes raising children feel possible and shared.** Subsidies can lower the price of care; they cannot supply the village. The frontier work therefore belongs to civil society and social enterprise: intergenerational co-housing where retirees and young families exchange childcare for companionship; employer coalitions that make the extended parental leave culturally safe to use rather than career suicide; neighbourhood care co-ops that turn the new subsidies into actual, walkable childcare capacity. Policy has opened a door; social innovators must build the rooms behind it.

### **Actionable Takeaways**

> * **For social entrepreneurs:** The new 12-year family-tenancy and childcare subsidies create demand for *care-integrated housing* — co-living models bundling secure homes with shared, subsidised childcare. This is a fundable, mission-aligned market.  
> * **For employers:** Extended leave only works if using it is normal. Build "leave-positive" cultures — publicly track uptake, protect promotion timelines, and let fathers lead by example.  
> * **For communities:** Convert subsidies into shared capacity through neighbourhood childcare co-ops and intergenerational time-banks, so support becomes relational, not merely financial.  
> * **The mindset shift:** Stop treating fertility as a purchase to be incentivised and start treating care as infrastructure to be built — collectively, visibly, and close to home.

## **\[繁中\] 設計一個「為下一代留出空間」的經濟**

### **引言**

本週，台灣「人口對策新戰略——家庭支持篇」的落地細節主導了生活話題，其中「居住減壓」支柱的「婚育宅／育嬰宅」邁向實施。這套涵蓋生育、托育、教育、友善職場與居住的 18 項措施，亮點鮮明：0 至 18 歲兒少每人每月 5,000 元「成長津貼」；育嬰假適用年齡從 3 歲延長至 6 歲、天數由 30 天倍增為 60 天；分胎次的托育補助最高每月 1.5 萬元；並為育兒家庭調降房屋稅與地價稅，讓育有 6 歲以下子女者可安心長住 12 年。面對全球數一數二的低生育率，台灣其實是在提問：一個社會能否「用設計」走出人口衰退？而這個問題的核心，正是最日常的生活——一對年輕夫妻能否買得起（住得穩）一個家，並敢於在其中養育一個孩子。

### **社會創新角度分析**

生育政策常被框成一場「補貼競標」——多發錢給父母，寄望多生孩子。但國際證據令人謙卑：光靠現金，鮮少能持久推升生育率。從社會創新觀察者的眼光看，這套方案有趣之處，在於它在何處「越過現金、走向重新設計照顧的基礎設施」。  
第一項創新，是把**「居住」與「養育」當成單一系統，而非兩個政策孤島。**將稅務減免與育兒家庭 12 年租期保障綁在一起，正面迎擊東亞低生育率背後真正的恐懼——不是買不起嬰兒推車，而是無法擁有安穩的棲身之所。社會創新長期主張「居住是其他一切人生決定的底層基質」；把安穩租期與養育綁定，是結構性的、而非表面性的一步。  
第二項，是**「成長津貼」本身的設計。**把 6 至 18 歲的給付切分為「一半自由運用、一半存入兒少專戶」，是一段安靜的行為設計——它培養資產累積的習慣，並讓每個年輕人擁有一小份對未來的「持股」。這呼應了全球「兒童發展帳戶」的實驗：即便微薄的資產持有，也能改變教育期待。創新在機制，而非金額。  
然而更鋒利的社會創新批判是：**國家方案無法製造生育真正仰賴的東西——一種讓「養小孩」感覺可行且被共同承擔的文化與社群。**補貼能降低照顧的價格，卻無法供應那座「一起帶孩子的村莊」。因此前沿工作屬於公民社會與社會企業：讓退休者與年輕家庭以「陪伴換托育」的跨世代共居；讓延長育嬰假在文化上「敢用」而非等於葬送職涯的雇主聯盟；把新補貼轉化為「走得到、用得到」托育量能的社區照顧合作社。政策打開了一扇門，社會創新者必須把門後的房間蓋出來。

### **可行的啟示與行動建議**

> * **對社會創業者：**新的 12 年育兒租期與托育補助，創造了「照顧整合型住宅」的需求——把安穩住所與共享、受補貼的托育綁在一起的共居模式。這是一個可被投資、且與使命相符的市場。  
> * **對雇主：**延長育嬰假唯有「敢用」才有效。打造「請假友善」文化——公開追蹤使用率、保障升遷時程，並讓父親帶頭示範。  
> * **對社區：**透過社區托育合作社與跨世代時間銀行，把補貼轉化為共享量能，讓支持成為關係性的，而非僅是財務性的。  
> * **心態轉變：**別再把生育當成「用誘因買來的一次消費」，而要把照顧當成「需要被集體、可見、就近打造的基礎設施」。

**Event 3｜行・樂 — The 48-Team 2026 World Cup and Its Carbon Reckoning**

## **\[EN\] Can the World's Biggest Party Learn to Tread Lightly?**

### **Introduction**

As the 2026 FIFA World Cup roared into its knockout stage this week — France edging Paraguay 1–0 into the quarter-finals on a Mbappé penalty — the tournament also became the centre of a very modern argument. This is the first 48-team World Cup: 104 matches across 16 host cities in three countries, projected to generate US\$30.5 billion in economic output and 185,000 US jobs. But a June 2026 study by carbon-accounting firm Greenly estimated its footprint at 7.8 million tonnes of CO₂ — more than double Qatar 2022 — with a staggering 87.8% coming from spectator and team travel across North America's vast distances. Add a fumbled ban on reusable water bottles during games played in peak afternoon heat, and the "beautiful game's" biggest-ever edition has become a live case study in whether mega-events can scale joy without scaling harm.

### **A Social-Innovation Reading**

The World Cup is one of humanity's great rituals of belonging — and precisely because it commands billions of eyeballs, it is a uniquely powerful platform for social innovation. The 2026 controversy crystallises a tension at the heart of every mega-event: **growth in reach versus growth in footprint.** A social-innovation response refuses the false choice and asks how to redesign the event itself.  
First, **travel is the problem, so travel is the design target.** With 88% of emissions from transit, the innovation lever is not stadium solar panels (a mere 3% of the footprint) but tournament *geography and scheduling*. Regional clustering of group-stage matches, rail-first fan corridors, and ticketing that bundles low-carbon transit are structural fixes. The deeper move is redefining a "successful" World Cup by fan-experience quality and community benefit rather than raw scale — challenging the growth logic that produced a 48-team format in the first place.  
Second, the reusable-bottle fiasco reveals **how sustainability fails when it is bolted on rather than co-designed.** Banning refillable bottles for "safety" while staging matches in dangerous heat is the opposite of human-centred design. The social innovation is participatory: co-design fan-welfare and waste systems *with* supporter groups, who consistently generate better ideas — refill stations, deposit-return cups, shade commons — than top-down edicts.  
Third, and most powerfully, is **the platform dividend.** A tournament watched by billions can normalise behaviours at civilisational scale. Imagine host cities using the event to launch permanent walkable "fan zones" that outlive the tournament as public space; refill and reuse systems that become city standards; or a transparent, published sustainability scorecard that turns each edition into a ratchet of improvement. The innovation is to treat the World Cup not as a one-month spectacle but as a *catalyst for durable local infrastructure and habits* — legacy measured in how cities live afterward, not in trophies.

### **Actionable Takeaways**

> * **For event organisers & cities:** Redesign for low-carbon travel first — regional match clustering, rail-bundled tickets, and fan zones engineered to become permanent public, walkable space after the final whistle.  
> * **For social entrepreneurs:** Mega-events are launchpads. Deploy reuse/refill and deposit-return systems at World Cup scale to prove them, then transfer the model to everyday city life.  
> * **For fans & supporter groups:** Demand co-design of welfare and waste policy; organised supporters are a proven engine of grassroots sustainability innovation.  
> * **The mindset shift:** Judge a mega-event not by how big it was, but by what it leaves behind — measure legacy in walkable streets, reuse habits and community assets, not attendance records.

## **\[繁中\] 全世界最大的派對，學得會「輕輕地走」嗎？**

### **引言**

本週 2026 世界盃轟然進入淘汰賽——法國以姆巴佩的一記 12 碼 1 比 0 力克巴拉圭挺進八強——這場賽事也同時成為一場極其現代的爭論核心。這是史上首屆 48 隊世界盃：橫跨三國 16 座主辦城市、共 104 場比賽，預估創造 305 億美元經濟產出與 18.5 萬個美國就業機會。然而，碳核算公司 Greenly 於 2026 年 6 月的研究估計，其碳足跡高達 780 萬公噸 CO₂——超過 2022 卡達世界盃的兩倍——而其中驚人的 87.8% 來自觀眾與球隊橫越北美遼闊距離的交通。再加上在午後高溫時段比賽卻一度禁帶可重複使用水瓶的荒腔走板，這場「美麗運動」史上最大的一屆，成了一則活生生的案例研究：大型盛會能否「放大歡愉，卻不放大傷害」？

### **社會創新角度分析**

世界盃是人類最偉大的「歸屬感儀式」之一——也正因它吸引數十億目光，它是威力獨一無二的社會創新平台。2026 年的爭議，凝結了每一場大型盛會核心的張力：**觸及範圍的成長，對上碳足跡的成長。**社會創新式的回應，拒絕這個假二選一，轉而追問：如何重新設計盛會本身？  
第一，**交通是問題所在，因此交通就是設計標的。**既然 88% 的排放來自交通，創新的槓桿就不是球場的太陽能板（僅佔足跡 3%），而是賽事的「地理與排程」。把小組賽依區域集中、以鐵路為先的球迷走廊、把低碳運輸綁進票券，都是結構性的解方。更深的一步，是以「球迷體驗品質與社區效益」而非「純粹規模」來重新定義「成功的世界盃」——正面挑戰那套一開始就催生出 48 隊制的成長邏輯。  
第二，可重複使用水瓶的鬧劇，揭示了**當永續是「事後外掛」而非「共同設計」時，會如何失敗。**以「安全」為由禁止可補充水瓶，卻在危險高溫下比賽，正是「以人為本設計」的反面。社會創新是參與式的：與球迷團體「一起」共同設計球迷福祉與廢棄物系統——他們往往比由上而下的命令，能生出更好的點子：補水站、押金回收杯、遮蔭公共空間。  
第三，也最有力的，是**「平台紅利」。**一場被數十億人觀看的賽事，能以文明尺度使某些行為「正常化」。想像主辦城市藉此推出可步行的「球迷專區」，並讓它在賽後長存為公共空間；讓補水與重複使用系統成為城市標準；或發布一份透明公開的永續計分卡，使每一屆都成為持續改善的「棘輪」。真正的創新，是把世界盃視為「耐久的在地基礎設施與習慣的催化劑」，而非一個月的奇觀——其遺產，衡量於城市在賽後如何生活，而非獎盃本身。

### **可行的啟示與行動建議**

> * **對賽事主辦方與城市：**以「低碳交通優先」重新設計——區域化賽程集中、鐵路綁定票券，並把球迷專區工程化為「終場後可長存的可步行公共空間」。  
> * **對社會創業者：**大型盛會是發射台。以世界盃的規模部署重複使用／補水與押金回收系統來驗證可行性，再把模式移轉到日常城市生活。  
> * **對球迷與球迷團體：**要求「共同設計」福祉與廢棄物政策；有組織的球迷，是草根永續創新的可靠引擎。  
> * **心態轉變：**評判一場大型盛會，不看它「有多大」，而看它「留下了什麼」——以可步行的街道、重複使用的習慣與社區資產來衡量遺產，而非入場人數紀錄。
