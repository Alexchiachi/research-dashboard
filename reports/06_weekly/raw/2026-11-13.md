## **English Draft**

### **When a Nation Resets Its Alarm Clock: Rethinking the School Day as Social Innovation**

#### **Introduction**

In the second week of November 2026, a quietly radical idea topped China's most-watched social platform: let children sleep a little longer. Schools across Jiangsu, Sichuan, Zhejiang and Chongqing announced that they would push back arrival times, shorten evening study sessions, and abolish the long-standing ritual of "morning reading." One primary school in Chengdu moved its start time to 8:40; a school in Chongqing has quietly run a 9:00 arrival pilot for nearly two years. The topic reached number one on Weibo's hot-search list on 8 November. What looks like a scheduling tweak is in fact a test case for how a society redesigns one of its most rigid institutions, the daily rhythm of childhood, and it deserves to be read as social innovation rather than mere administration.

#### **A Social Innovation Lens**

Social innovation is usually defined as a new solution to a social problem that is more effective, efficient or just than existing approaches, and that creates value for society as a whole rather than for private interests. The delayed-start movement fits that definition almost exactly. The social problem is well documented: adolescent sleep deprivation is linked to depression, weakened immunity, poorer memory consolidation and higher accident rates. The existing approach, an arms race of ever-earlier mornings, optimised for the appearance of diligence while quietly eroding the health it claimed to serve. The innovation is not a new technology but a new default, and defaults are among the most powerful levers a society has.  
What makes this episode genuinely innovative, rather than a simple policy order handed down from above, is its texture. It is emerging unevenly, school by school and city by city, each institution adjusting the dials differently: 8:00 here, 8:40 there, 9:00 in a two-year pilot elsewhere. This is the signature of social innovation in practice. It behaves less like a law and more like a distributed experiment, where many local trials generate evidence that can be compared, refined and scaled. The Chongqing pilot matters precisely because it has run long enough to show that a later start does not cause the sky to fall.  
The friction surfacing in the public conversation is equally instructive. Parents who work fixed shifts point out that a later school start collides with their own commute, turning a child-health reform into a childcare gap. Others argue that shortening after-school detention would help more than delaying the morning. These objections are not reasons to abandon the change; they are the raw material of good design. They reveal that the school day is not an isolated system but a node in a web that includes labour hours, commuting infrastructure, and the economics of two-income households. A reform that moves one node without accounting for the others simply relocates the stress. True social innovation treats these complaints as a specification, not as noise.  
There is also a quieter shift in values beneath the logistics. For decades, the morning reading session functioned as a cultural symbol of effort, a visible proof that a child, and by extension a family and a school, was trying hard enough. Retiring it is a small act of permission: the admission that rest is not the opposite of achievement but a precondition for it. Social innovations often work by changing what a community considers normal and admirable, and this one is renegotiating the meaning of diligence itself.

#### **Implications and Actionable Takeaways**

For education administrators, the lesson is to treat the schedule as an adjustable system and to publish the evidence. Pilots like Chongqing's should be documented transparently, with sleep, attendance, grades and wellbeing tracked over time, so that later adopters inherit data rather than guesswork. A shared, open dashboard of outcomes would turn scattered trials into a genuine learning network.  
For reformers anywhere, the actionable principle is to redesign the whole chain, not a single link. If the morning moves later, the after-school hours, the commute, and the parent's workday must move with it. Pairing a later start with employer-side flexibility, safe early "drop-off rooms" for working parents, or staggered community schedules would prevent the reform from merely shifting the burden onto households.  
For families and civil society, the opportunity is to stay in the conversation and supply the local knowledge that top-down planning lacks. The parents raising objections this week are, whether they intend it or not, acting as co-designers. The most durable version of this reform will be one that treats their constraints as the problem to solve. A society that can learn to let its children sleep, and to rebuild the surrounding systems so that sleep is actually possible, is practising social innovation in its most humane form.

### **當一個社會重設鬧鐘：把「上學時間」當成社會創新來思考**

#### **引言**

2026 年 11 月第二週，一個看似溫和卻相當激進的念頭登上了中國大陸最受矚目的社群平台：讓孩子多睡一點。江蘇、四川、浙江、重慶多地的中小學陸續宣布延後到校時間、縮短晚自習，並取消行之有年的「早讀」。成都一所小學自立冬起把到校時間改為 8:40，重慶巴南一所小學低年級 9:00 到校的試點更已默默運作近兩年。這個話題在 11 月 8 日衝上微博熱搜第一。表面上只是作息微調，實際上卻是一個社會如何重新設計其中一項最僵固的制度——童年的日常節奏——的試驗場，值得用「社會創新」而非單純的行政命令來閱讀。

#### **社會創新角度分析**

社會創新通常被定義為：針對某個社會問題，提出比既有做法更有效、更合理或更公平的新解方，而其所創造的價值歸於整體社會，而非少數私利。延後上學的行動幾乎完全符合這個定義。社會問題證據明確：青少年長期睡眠不足與憂鬱、免疫力下降、記憶鞏固變差、意外事故率升高都有關聯。既有做法是一場「比誰更早」的軍備競賽，表面成全了勤奮的樣貌，卻悄悄侵蝕了它聲稱要守護的健康。這項創新靠的不是新科技，而是一個新的「預設值」——而預設值，正是一個社會手上最有力的槓桿之一。  
讓這起事件真正具有創新意義、而不只是由上而下一紙命令的，是它的質地。它是不均勻地、一校一城地冒出來的，每個學校把旋鈕調到不同刻度：這裡 8:00、那裡 8:40、另一地則是跑了兩年的 9:00 試點。這正是社會創新在現場的特徵。它比較不像一條法律，而更像一場分散式實驗——眾多在地試點產生可以互相比較、修正並放大的證據。重慶的試點之所以重要，恰恰在於它跑得夠久，足以證明晚一點上學，天並不會塌下來。  
公共討論裡浮現的摩擦同樣值得玩味。輪班固定的上班族家長指出，延後上學與自己的通勤時間相撞，使一項顧及孩子健康的改革，變成了照顧上的空窗；也有人主張，與其延後早上，不如先縮短課後的延時留校。這些反對並非放棄改革的理由，而是良好設計的原料。它們揭示出：上學時間不是一個孤立系統，而是鑲嵌在一張網裡的節點，這張網包含了勞動工時、通勤基礎設施，以及雙薪家庭的經濟現實。只動一個節點、卻不顧其他節點的改革，只會把壓力搬家。真正的社會創新會把這些抱怨當成「規格書」，而不是雜訊。  
在物流與作息之下，還有一層更安靜的價值轉變。數十年來，「早讀」扮演著努力的文化象徵，是一個孩子、進而是一個家庭與一所學校「夠拚」的可見證明。讓它退場，是一個微小的「允許」：承認休息並不是成就的反面，而是成就的前提。社會創新往往透過改變一個群體認為「正常而值得敬佩」的事物來起作用，而這一次，它正在重新協商「勤奮」這個詞本身的意義。

#### **可行啟示與行動建議**

對教育主管機關而言，啟示是把作息當成一個可調整的系統，並把證據公開。像重慶這樣的試點應被透明地記錄，長期追蹤睡眠、出勤、成績與身心健康，讓後來的採用者繼承的是數據，而非猜測。一個共享、開放的成效儀表板，能把零散的試點變成真正的學習網絡。  
對任何地方的改革者而言，可操作的原則是：重新設計整條鏈，而不是單一環節。若早上延後，課後時段、通勤與家長的工作日都必須跟著挪動。把延後上學搭配雇主端的彈性工時、為上班族家長設置安全的「提早托管室」，或錯開社區作息，才能避免改革只是把負擔轉嫁到家庭身上。  
對家庭與公民社會而言，機會在於留在對話中，提供由上而下規劃所缺乏的在地知識。本週提出異議的家長，無論是否自覺，其實都在扮演「共同設計者」。這項改革最耐久的版本，會是把他們的限制當成「待解問題」的那一個版本。一個能學會讓孩子睡覺、並重建周邊系統讓睡眠真正成為可能的社會，正在以最有人味的形式，實踐社會創新。

## **English Draft**

### **The Fur on the Hood: How a Boycott Became a Design Brief for Ethical Fashion**

#### **Introduction**

In early November 2026, a down jacket trimmed with a detachable collar of "100% natural fox fur," priced near NT\$15,000, turned a Taiwanese influencer fashion brand into the center of an online storm. Customers who had admired the label felt betrayed when they learned the trim was real animal fur rather than the faux material many had assumed. The backlash was swift enough that on 10 November the brand, aeom, issued a statement promising to stop using real animal fur in future products and to offer unconditional returns to anyone uneasy about what they had bought. In mainland China the same week, a women's-wear shop faced a parallel scandal over cat fur passed off as fox. Stripped of outrage, both episodes are really about something hopeful: consumers writing a design brief that the industry is now being forced to read.

#### **A Social Innovation Lens**

It is tempting to file this under consumer anger and move on. A social innovation lens sees more. Social innovation often begins when a previously invisible cost, borne by someone or something with no voice in the transaction, is suddenly made visible and a community decides it is no longer acceptable. For most of fashion's history, the suffering of a farmed animal was external to the price tag. What the boycott did was internalise it, forcing the brand to price in a moral cost that the market had long ignored. That is the same mechanism by which societies once reformed child labour and sweatshops: not through a single law, but through a shift in what buyers refuse to tolerate.  
The most telling detail is that many customers assumed the trim was faux. That assumption is itself evidence of a social innovation that has already succeeded. A decade ago, real fur signalled luxury; today, a large segment of consumers defaults to expecting a cruelty-free alternative and feels deceived when reality differs. Faux fur, recycled down, plant-based leathers and similar material innovations have quietly rewired expectations, so that the humane option is now the imagined norm. The outrage was not just ethical; it was the shock of a norm being violated.  
Equally important is the speed and shape of the response. The brand did not merely apologise; it committed to a material policy change and, crucially, offered unconditional returns. Whatever its motives, this is the market behaving as a feedback loop, with consumer pressure translating directly into a product-design decision within days. The near-simultaneous Chinese case, where a factory allegedly substituted cat fur for fox, exposes the other half of the problem: opaque supply chains in which even the brand may not fully know what it is selling. Social innovation in fashion therefore has two fronts. One is values, deciding that animal fur is no longer worth its cost. The other is infrastructure, building the traceability and labelling systems that let a value actually be enforced. A boycott can win the first; only transparent supply chains can secure the second.  
There is a risk worth naming. Outrage can curdle into performative shaming that punishes a single brand while leaving the system untouched, or it can shift demand toward cheaper synthetic furs with their own environmental costs. The constructive version of this energy channels anger into durable structures: certification schemes, honest labelling, and support for genuinely sustainable materials rather than a simple swap of one harm for another.

#### **Implications and Actionable Takeaways**

For brands, the clearest takeaway is to treat material sourcing as a transparency problem, not a marketing afterthought. Publishing a verifiable material policy, auditing suppliers against it, and labelling honestly would turn the reactive apology of this week into a proactive advantage. The Chinese cat-fur case shows that "we didn't know what our factory did" is no longer an acceptable answer.  
For consumers and advocacy groups, the lesson is to convert episodic boycotts into standing systems. A boycott fades; a trusted certification mark, a public materials database, or a shared "cruelty-free" labelling standard keeps working after the news cycle ends. Directing attention toward these tools multiplies the impact of a single week's anger.  
For policymakers and platforms, there is room to mandate clear material disclosure for garments sold online, so that "I thought it was faux" can never again be an accident of ambiguous listings. When the humane choice is already what most buyers expect, the role of good design, honest labelling and enforceable standards is simply to let people buy what they already believe they are buying.

### **帽緣上的那圈毛：一場抵制，如何變成倫理時尚的設計需求書**

#### **引言**

2026 年 11 月初，一件領口鑲著「100% 天然狐狸毛」可拆卸飾邊、單價近新台幣 1.5 萬元的羽絨外套，把台灣一個網紅時尚品牌推上了網路風暴中心。許多原本欣賞該品牌的消費者，在得知那圈毛是真動物皮草、而非他們以為的人造材質後，感到被背叛。反彈來得又快又猛，品牌 aeom 於 11 月 10 日發出聲明，承諾未來商品不再使用真動物皮草，並對任何對材質有疑慮的買家提供無條件退換貨。同一週，中國大陸一家女裝網店也爆出以貓毛冒充狐狸毛的平行醜聞。若撇開憤怒本身，這兩起事件其實關乎一件更令人樂觀的事：消費者正在撰寫一份「設計需求書」，而產業如今被迫要讀懂它。

#### **社會創新角度分析**

把這件事歸類為「消費者發火」然後翻篇，是很容易的。但社會創新的視角看見更多。社會創新往往始於一個時刻：某個過去隱形、由交易中沒有發言權的人或生命所承擔的成本，突然被攤在陽光下，而一個群體決定不再接受它。在時尚史的大部分時間裡，一隻養殖動物的痛苦，對價格標籤而言是「外部」的。抵制所做的，就是把它「內部化」，逼品牌把市場長期忽略的道德成本計入定價。這與社會曾經改革童工與血汗工廠的機制如出一轍：靠的不是單一法律，而是買家「拒絕容忍什麼」的集體轉變。  
最能說明問題的細節，是許多消費者原本以為那是人造毛。這個「以為」本身，就是一項已經成功的社會創新的證據。十年前，真皮草象徵奢華；今天，一大群消費者的「預設期待」是無殘酷的替代材質，當現實不符時便覺得受騙。人造毛、再生羽絨、植物基皮革等材料創新，已經悄悄重寫了期待，使「人道選項」成為被想像的常態。這場憤怒不只是倫理上的，更是一個常規被違反時的錯愕。  
同樣重要的，是回應的速度與形狀。品牌不只是道歉，更承諾了材質政策的改變，並關鍵性地提供無條件退換貨。無論動機為何，這都是市場作為一個「回饋迴路」在運作——消費者壓力在數日之內，直接轉譯成一項產品設計決策。幾乎同時發生的中國案例，工廠疑以貓毛替代狐狸毛，則揭露了問題的另一半：不透明的供應鏈，連品牌自己可能都不完全清楚賣的是什麼。因此，時尚業的社會創新有兩條戰線。一條是價值，決定動物皮草不再值得它的代價；另一條是基礎建設，打造可追溯與標示系統，讓一個價值真正能被落實。抵制能贏下第一條，唯有透明供應鏈能守住第二條。  
有個風險值得點名。憤怒可能酸化成「表演式公審」，懲罰了單一品牌，卻讓整個系統原封不動；也可能把需求推向更便宜、卻有自身環境代價的合成毛皮。這股能量的建設性版本，是把憤怒導入耐久的結構：認證制度、誠實標示，以及對真正永續材料的支持，而不是用一種傷害簡單換掉另一種傷害。

#### **可行啟示與行動建議**

對品牌而言，最清楚的啟示是：把材料來源當成一個「透明度問題」，而非行銷的事後補充。公開一份可驗證的材質政策、依此稽核供應商、並誠實標示，能把本週被動的道歉，轉為主動的優勢。中國的貓毛案證明，「我們不知道工廠做了什麼」已不再是可被接受的答案。  
對消費者與倡議團體而言，啟示是把「偶發的抵制」轉化為「常設的系統」。抵制會退潮，但一個受信任的認證標章、一個公開的材料資料庫，或一套共享的「無殘酷」標示標準，會在新聞週期結束後繼續運作。把注意力導向這些工具，能讓一週憤怒的影響力加倍。  
對政策制定者與平台而言，有空間要求線上販售的服飾清楚揭露材質，讓「我以為是人造的」不再因模糊的商品描述而發生。當人道選擇已經是多數買家的預期，良好設計、誠實標示與可執行標準所要做的，不過是讓人們買到他們本就相信自己買到的東西。

### **The Vanishing Oden Pot: What a Convenience-Store Snack Reveals About Food Waste and Innovation**

#### **Introduction**

As the first real cold of November 2026 settled over Taiwan, people reached for a familiar comfort, the steaming pot of oden (guandongzhu) by the convenience-store counter, only to find it increasingly gone. The disappearance became a minor national conversation this week, with shoppers puzzled and store insiders explaining the economics: high spoilage, costly broth and consumables, thin margins, and the daily labour of scrubbing the equipment. Many outlets have quietly swapped the open simmering pot for microwavable hot-pot packs, eggs, corn and sweet potatoes, items that waste less and earn more. No chain has released official sales data. Yet behind this small culinary loss sits a question at the heart of social innovation: how a society feeds itself at the margins, and what it throws away to do so.

#### **A Social Innovation Lens**

The oden pot is, in systems terms, a beautiful and wasteful object. Dozens of items simmer in shared broth, held hot for hours in the hope that someone will buy them before they must be binned. Store insiders describe pots emptied into the trash at the end of a slow day. This is the classic tension that social innovation in the food system exists to resolve: the conflict between availability, the promise that a hot meal is always ready, and waste, the cost of keeping that promise. When a retailer retreats from oden, it is not simply abandoning a snack; it is making a rational decision that the current design of "always-on hot food" is too lossy to sustain. The interesting question is not why the pots are vanishing, but whether a better design could bring them back.  
Framed this way, the humble oden counter becomes a laboratory for several live strands of food innovation. Demand forecasting, increasingly aided by data and even machine learning, lets some retailers cook closer to real need and cut end-of-day disposal. Dynamic discounting, marking down perishable food as closing time approaches, converts would-be waste into affordable meals, a practice that platforms built around surplus food have scaled across the world. The shift toward microwavable packs that the stores are choosing is itself an innovation of a conservative kind: portion-controlled, long-life, low-spoilage. It solves the waste problem, but at a cost to the sensory and social texture of food, trading the communal steam of a shared pot for a sealed tray. Social innovation asks whether we can get the waste reduction without surrendering the warmth.  
There is also a quieter equity dimension. Convenience-store hot food is, for many shift workers, students and lower-income city dwellers, an accessible warm meal at an affordable price, available at hours when little else is open. When the most waste-prone but also most affordable option retreats, the question of who still has easy access to a cheap hot meal at midnight becomes a real one. A purely cost-driven solution, cut the lossy item, risks solving the retailer's accounting problem while shrinking the food safety net for the people who relied on it. The social-innovation version of the same problem asks how to keep the access while fixing the economics.  
Finally, the episode is a reminder that waste is rarely the fault of a careless individual; it is designed into a system. The spoilage of the oden pot is not caused by wasteful shoppers but by a business model that must keep food hot on the chance of a sale. Redesign the model, through better forecasting, redistribution of surplus, or new preservation methods, and the waste changes with it. That reframing, from blaming behaviour to redesigning systems, is one of the core moves of social innovation.

#### **Implications and Actionable Takeaways**

For retailers, the actionable path is to treat spoilage as a design target rather than an unavoidable cost. Pairing better demand forecasting with end-of-day dynamic discounting could preserve the beloved oden pot while shrinking the losses that are driving it out. A marked-down pot at 10 p.m. feeds a hungry student and wastes nothing.  
For social entrepreneurs and food-rescue platforms, convenience stores represent a large, distributed stream of near-expiry hot food that is currently binned. Partnerships that route this surplus to discounted sale, food-sharing apps, or charitable redistribution turn a waste stream into both meals and margin, a textbook social-innovation win-win.  
For policymakers and communities, the disappearing oden pot is a prompt to ask who the food system's convenience tier actually serves, and to protect affordable late-night nutrition as a quiet piece of social infrastructure. The goal is not nostalgia for a particular snack, but a food system that can be warm, accessible and low-waste at the same time. That is the standard social innovation sets, and the empty space where the oden pot stood is a good place to start meeting it.

### **消失的關東煮：一鍋超商小吃，照見食物浪費與創新**

#### **引言**

2026 年 11 月，入冬第一波真正的冷空氣籠罩台灣，人們伸手想抓住一份熟悉的慰藉——超商櫃檯旁那鍋熱氣蒸騰的關東煮——卻發現它愈來愈難找到。這份「消失」本週成了一樁小小的全民話題：消費者困惑，門市內行人則點出背後的經濟帳：報廢率高、湯底與耗材成本高、利潤薄，還要天天刷洗機具。不少門市悄悄用微波加熱鍋物，或蛋、玉米、地瓜取代了那鍋敞開的滾湯——這些品項浪費更少、利潤更高。目前沒有任何連鎖超商公布官方銷售數據。然而，在這樁小小的飲食失落背後，坐著一個社會創新的核心問題：一個社會如何在邊角處餵飽自己，又為此丟掉了什麼。

#### **社會創新角度分析**

以系統的眼光看，關東煮那一鍋是個既美麗又浪費的物件。數十種食材在共用的湯底裡滾著，被保溫數小時，只盼在必須報廢之前有人買走。門市內行人形容，生意清淡的一天結束時，整鍋倒進垃圾桶。這正是食物系統的社會創新存在的經典張力：「供應」——隨時都有熱食可取的承諾——與「浪費」——維持這個承諾的代價——之間的衝突。當零售商從關東煮撤退，它並不只是放棄一樣小吃，而是做出一個理性判斷：目前「永遠熱著的熱食」這套設計太耗損、難以為繼。有趣的問題不是「鍋為什麼消失」，而是「一套更好的設計能否讓它回來」。  
這樣一框，那個不起眼的關東煮檯，就成了好幾股活躍的食物創新的實驗室。需求預測——愈來愈倚賴數據甚至機器學習——讓部分零售商能更貼近真實需求去烹煮，削減打烊前的報廢。動態折扣——在接近打烊時把即期食品降價——把本將成為垃圾的食物，變成人人買得起的餐點，這套做法已被全球以剩食為核心的平台規模化。超商正在選擇的「改賣微波鍋物」本身，也是一種保守型的創新：定量、長效、低報廢。它解決了浪費，卻犧牲了食物的感官與社交質地，用一盤密封餐盒，換掉了一鍋共享的蒸氣。社會創新要問的是：我們能不能在不交出那份溫熱的前提下，拿到減廢的成果。  
這裡還有一個較安靜的「公平」面向。對許多輪班勞工、學生與都市中低收入者而言，超商熱食是一頓負擔得起、在其他店家都打烊的時段仍買得到的溫熱餐。當最會報廢、卻也最平價的那個選項撤退，「誰還能在午夜輕鬆買到一頓便宜熱食」就成了真實的問題。純粹由成本驅動的解法——砍掉耗損品項——有可能解決了零售商的會計難題，卻縮小了那些仰賴它的人的「食物安全網」。同一問題的社會創新版本，則會問：如何在修好經濟帳的同時，保住這份可及性。  
最後，這樁事件提醒我們：浪費很少是某個不經心個人的錯，而是被設計進系統裡的。關東煮的報廢，並非出於浪費的消費者，而是出於一套「必須為了一筆可能的買賣而把食物一直熱著」的商業模式。重新設計這套模式——透過更好的預測、剩食的再分配，或新的保存方法——浪費就會隨之改變。這種從「責怪行為」轉向「重新設計系統」的視角切換，正是社會創新最核心的動作之一。

#### **可行啟示與行動建議**

對零售商而言，可操作的路徑是把報廢當成一個「設計目標」，而非無可避免的成本。把更好的需求預測與打烊前的動態折扣搭配起來，就能在縮減逼退它的損耗的同時，保住那鍋人見人愛的關東煮。晚上十點一鍋降價的關東煮，餵飽一個飢餓的學生，又一點都不浪費。  
對社會企業與剩食平台而言，超商代表著一股龐大而分散、目前被丟棄的即期熱食來源。把這些剩食導向折扣銷售、惜食 App 或公益再分配的合作，能把一條「浪費流」同時變成餐點與利潤，是教科書等級的社會創新雙贏。  
對政策制定者與社區而言，消失的關東煮是一個提問的契機：食物系統的「便利層」究竟服務了誰？並把平價的深夜營養當成一塊安靜的社會基礎設施加以守護。目標不是對某樣小吃的懷舊，而是一套能同時做到溫熱、可及與低浪費的食物系統。這正是社會創新所設下的標準，而關東煮曾經站立、如今空出的那塊位置，是開始達成它的好起點。
