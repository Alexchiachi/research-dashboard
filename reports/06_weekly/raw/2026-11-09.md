**熱點一／Topic 1：台北市「廢行動電源回收換超商卡」**

Taipei's Power-Bank Recycling-for-Rewards Drive（行・住／E-waste・循環經濟）

**來源連結 / Sources：** [數位時代 Bnext](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/92843/taipei-power-bank-recycling-2026)； [NOWnews 今日新聞](https://www.nownews.com/news/6757319)； [今周刊 Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.com.tw/article/category/183030/post/202611020011/)

### **【English Draft】Turning Dead Batteries into Civic Currency: What Taipei's Power-Bank Buy-Back Teaches Us About Social Innovation**

**Introduction.** For one week in early November 2026 (November 2–8), Taipei's Department of Environmental Protection ran a deceptively small campaign: bring three dead power banks to one of sixty designated electronics stores — Tsann Kuen 3C, Senao, or Deyi Digital — and walk away with a NT\$50 convenience-store gift card. The city capped the giveaway at 3,600 cards across the metropolis, and they vanished fast. On the surface this is a minor consumer promotion. Looked at through the lens of social innovation, it is a small but instructive experiment in how a city can redesign the relationship between citizens, waste, and value.  
**Why this is a social-innovation story, not just a recycling story.** Social innovation is usually defined as new solutions — products, services, or models — that simultaneously meet a social need and create new social relationships or collaborations. Lithium-ion power banks are a textbook "wicked" waste problem: they are ubiquitous, they degrade invisibly, they are a genuine fire hazard in general trash and recycling trucks, and most households have no idea where to take them. The conventional policy answer is a rule ("do not throw batteries in the bin") backed by a fine — a prohibition that depends on guilt and enforcement. Taipei's move inverts the logic. Instead of punishing the wrong behaviour, it rewards the right one, and it does so by routing the reward through two existing networks citizens already trust: 3C retail chains and convenience stores. No new infrastructure was built. The innovation is relational and organisational, not technological.  
**The design choices worth noticing.** Three features make the scheme more than a gimmick. First, *friction reduction*: the drop-off points are places people already pass — electronics stores they visit to buy the next gadget — so the "cost" of doing the right thing collapses. Second, *immediate, legible reward*: a NT\$50 gift card is small, but it is instant and universally useful, which behavioural research shows beats a larger but abstract environmental benefit. Third, *cross-sector choreography*: the city supplied the mandate and the reward budget; private retailers supplied the physical access, staff, and a parallel trade-in discount (up to NT\$500 off new devices). This is the "collective impact" pattern — public, private, and citizen actors each doing the part they are structurally best placed to do.  
**The limits — and why they matter.** A clear-eyed analysis must note the weaknesses, because they are where the next innovation lives. The 3,600-card cap meant the programme sold out quickly, converting a public-good campaign into a mini treasure hunt that may have rewarded the already-informed and quick rather than reaching new behaviours. A one-week window builds no habit. And a reward-driven model risks "crowding out" intrinsic motivation: once people recycle for a gift card, do they stop recycling when the cards run out? The deeper structural gap is upstream. Buying back dead batteries treats the symptom; the disease is a product category engineered for disposability, sold cheaply as conference swag and festival giveaways, with no deposit built into its price. Genuine systemic social innovation would push the cost of end-of-life back onto producers — an extended producer responsibility (EPR) deposit baked into every power bank at point of sale.  
**Actionable insights.** For *city governments*: pilots like this are valuable precisely because they are cheap and fast, but their job is to generate evidence, not applause — pair every giveaway with a simple before/after survey and publish the collection tonnage so the public can see the social return. For *retailers and brands*: the smartest play is to make take-back permanent and ambient (a battery bin by every checkout) rather than a one-week event, turning goodwill into a standing service. For *social entrepreneurs*: the white space is a *deposit-return system for small lithium devices* — a QR-coded power bank that carries a refundable deposit, closing the loop at design stage. For *citizens*: the quiet lesson is that convenience, not conscience, is the real lever; the schemes that change behaviour are the ones that meet us where we already are. Taipei's week-long experiment will not solve the e-waste crisis, but it models the right instinct — design the easy path to be the responsible one, and let a whole city rehearse a better habit, even if only for seven days.

### **【繁體中文初稿】讓廢電池變成「公民貨幣」：從台北行動電源回收看社會創新**

**引言。**2026 年 11 月 2 日至 8 日，台北市環保局推出一項看似微小的活動：民眾只要把三顆廢棄行動電源帶到燦坤 3C、神腦國際或德誼數位等六十間指定 3C 門市，就能換得一張 50 元超商商品卡。全市限量 3,600 張，很快就被換光。乍看之下，這不過是一次普通的消費促銷；但若換上「社會創新」的眼鏡來看，它其實是一場小而關鍵的實驗——一座城市如何重新設計「市民、廢棄物與價值」三者之間的關係。  
**為什麼這是社會創新，而不只是回收。**社會創新通常被定義為：能同時滿足社會需求、又創造出新的社會關係或協作模式的新方案。鋰電池行動電源正是典型的「棘手廢棄物」：它無所不在、電芯老化卻看不見、混入一般垃圾或資源回收車時是真實的火災風險，而多數家庭根本不知道該把它丟去哪裡。傳統政策的答案是「規定＋罰則」——禁止你亂丟，靠罪惡感與稽查來維持。台北的做法把邏輯倒過來：不懲罰錯的行為，而是獎勵對的行為，並且讓這份獎勵，穿過市民原本就信任的兩張既有網絡——3C 連鎖與超商。沒有蓋任何新設施，創新發生在「關係」與「組織」層面，而非技術。  
**值得注意的設計巧思。**有三個特徵，讓這個方案不只是噱頭。第一是*降低摩擦*：回收點就設在人們本來就會經過的 3C 門市，做對的事幾乎不需額外成本。第二是*即時且看得懂的回饋*：50 元商品卡金額不大，但即時、通用，行為科學早已指出，這比「抽象而遙遠的環境效益」更能驅動行動。第三是*跨部門協奏*：城市提供政策與獎勵預算，民間零售商提供實體通路、人力，以及同步的舊換新折抵（指定商品最高折 500 元）。這正是「集體影響力（collective impact）」的樣貌——公部門、企業與市民，各自做自己結構上最擅長的那一塊。  
**侷限——以及它們為何重要。**誠實的分析必須指出弱點，因為下一個創新往往就藏在那裡。3,600 張的上限使活動迅速完售，把一場公共財行動變成「尋寶遊戲」，可能只獎勵到了原本就懂、手腳又快的人，而非真正帶動新行為。為期一週，養不出習慣。而「靠獎勵」的模式也有「排擠內在動機」的風險：當人們為商品卡而回收，卡發完了，他們會不會也就不回收了？更深的結構缺口在上游——回收廢電池處理的是症狀，病根卻是一整類被設計成「用完即丟」的產品：便宜地當作展場、活動贈品大量送出，售價裡從未內含任何押金。真正具系統性的社會創新，會把「報廢成本」推回生產者身上——也就是在每顆行動電源售出時，就內建一筆「生產者延伸責任（EPR）」押金。  
**可行的啟示與行動建議。**對*城市政府*：這類試辦之所以有價值，正因為它便宜又快，但它的任務是「產出證據」而非「收割掌聲」——每次活動都該搭配簡單的前後問卷，並公開回收重量，讓市民看見社會投資報酬。對*零售商與品牌*：最聰明的做法是把回收「常態化、環境化」（每個收銀台旁都放一個電池回收盒），而非辦一週的活動，把善意變成一項長存的服務。對*社會創業者*：留白處是「小型鋰電裝置的押金返還制度」——一顆帶 QR 碼、附可退押金的行動電源，在設計階段就把循環閉環。對*市民*：安靜的啟示是——真正的槓桿是「便利」而非「良心」；能改變行為的方案，都是那些願意來到我們原本所在之處的方案。台北這場為期七天的實驗不會解決電子廢棄物危機，但它示範了對的直覺：把「容易的路」設計成「負責任的路」，讓一整座城市，哪怕只有七天，也排練了一個更好的習慣。

**熱點二／Topic 2：比利時根特「蔬食星期四」**

Ghent's "Veggie Thursdays"（食／永續飲食・草根公民運動）

**來源連結 / Sources：** [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/europe/travel/europes-most-wholesome-city-break)； [Dialogue Earth](https://dialogue.earth/en/uncategorized/3012-vegetarian-thursdays-for-belgian-city/)； [Good Good Good（本週好消息）](https://goodgoodgood.co/articles/good-news-this-week-november-7-2026)

### **【English Draft】A City That Eats Differently on Thursdays: Ghent and the Social Innovation of the "Default"**

**Introduction.** This week Ghent resurfaced in travel and good-news roundups as "Europe's veggie capital," a label built on a civic experiment that is now old enough to judge. Since 2009, the Belgian city of roughly 260,000 people has observed *Donderdag Veggiedag* — "Thursday Veggie Day" — a voluntary weekly invitation for restaurants, canteens, and schools to put plant-based food at the centre of the plate. It began not in city hall but in a grassroots organisation, Ethical Vegetarian Alternatives (EVA), and the city joined as a partner rather than a commander. More than a decade on, the question is not "is it cute?" but "what did it actually change, and what can other places copy?"  
**The social-innovation core: redesigning the default, not the rule.** The genius of Veggie Day is what it is *not*. It is not a ban, not a tax, not a mandate. No one in Ghent is forbidden meat on Thursday. What changed is the *default*: in participating public canteens and schools, the vegetarian option became the one you receive unless you opt out. Behavioural science is unambiguous that defaults are among the most powerful levers we have, precisely because they respect freedom while reshaping the easy choice. This is social innovation at the level of *architecture of choice* rather than exhortation. It sidesteps the culture war over whether people *should* eat less meat and simply makes eating less meat the frictionless path one day a week. A meat-loving city could accept it because nothing was taken away — a plate was merely rearranged.  
**Why the grassroots origin matters.** Had the mayor decreed "Meatless Thursdays," it would likely have triggered backlash and a freedom-of-the-plate counter-movement. Instead the idea was carried by a trusted civil-society organisation and *offered* to institutions, with the city lending legitimacy, a map of participating restaurants, and cooking workshops. This is a hallmark of durable social innovation: the state does not impose the behaviour, it *scaffolds* an ecosystem in which the new norm can spread horizontally — restaurant to restaurant, parent to parent. The result is resilience. Policies imposed top-down die with the administration that passed them; norms grown through civil society outlive election cycles, which is precisely why Ghent's Thursday habit is still here, and still exportable, seventeen years later.  
**A clear-eyed reading of the evidence.** Honesty requires caution about the numbers. Widely circulated figures — "half the city participates," high flexitarian rates — come from enthusiastic single sources and should be treated as directional, not audited. What is more defensible is the *mechanism*: a low-cost, opt-out, partnership-based nudge that normalised a behaviour and, by the city's own framing, supports its goal of climate neutrality by 2050\. The deeper structural limit is that voluntary one-day nudges cannot, alone, shift an entire food system whose economics still favour cheap meat. Veggie Day is a cultural on-ramp, not a destination. Its real value may be less in the tonnes of carbon saved and more in the quiet proof that a mainstream city can renegotiate a dietary norm without coercion.  
**Actionable insights.** For *municipalities and institutions* (hospitals, universities, large employers): the transferable move is not "promote vegetarianism" but "change one default in one canteen on one day," measure uptake and plate waste, and let success make the argument. For *civil-society groups*: Ghent shows the power of being the *offerer* rather than the protester — bring institutions a ready-made, low-risk package (recipes, supplier lists, signage) and let them say yes. For *restaurants and food brands*: a designated "veggie day" is a marketing asset and a kitchen R\&D prompt, not a sacrifice. For *individuals and families*: the lesson scales down to the household — you do not need to become vegetarian to change your footprint; you need to change one default, one day, and let the new normal do the rest. Ghent's most exportable product was never a recipe. It was a method: shift the default, keep the freedom, let the norm travel.

### **【繁體中文初稿】一座城市在星期四吃得不一樣：根特與「預設值」的社會創新**

**引言。**本週，比利時根特市再度登上旅遊與「好消息」榜單，被稱為「歐洲蔬食之都」——這個名號，建立在一場如今已足夠成熟、可供檢驗的公民實驗上。自 2009 年起，這座約 26 萬人的城市便推行「星期四蔬食日（Donderdag Veggiedag）」：每週一天，自願性地邀請餐廳、公共食堂與學校，把植物性飲食放到餐盤的中心。它的起點不是市政廳，而是一個草根組織「道德蔬食替代方案（EVA）」；城市是以「夥伴」而非「指揮官」的身分加入的。十多年後，該問的已不是「這點子可不可愛」，而是「它到底改變了什麼、其他地方又能複製什麼」。  
**社會創新的核心：重新設計「預設值」，而不是「規則」。**蔬食日的高明之處，在於它「不是」什麼：它不是禁令、不是課稅、不是強制。根特沒有任何人在星期四被禁止吃肉。真正改變的是*預設值*——在參與的公共食堂與學校裡，蔬食餐成了「你不特別說，就會拿到的那一份」。行為科學早有定論：預設值是我們手上最強大的槓桿之一，正因為它一邊尊重選擇自由、一邊重塑了「最省力的選項」。這是發生在*選擇架構*層次的社會創新，而非道德勸說。它巧妙繞開了「人們該不該少吃肉」的文化戰爭，單純地把「少吃肉」變成每週一天最無摩擦的那條路。一座愛吃肉的城市之所以能接受，是因為什麼都沒被拿走——只是一個餐盤被重新排列了。  
**草根起源為何關鍵。**如果當初是市長一聲令下「星期四禁肉」，很可能會引爆反彈，激起一場「餐盤自由」的反制運動。但這個點子是由一個受信任的公民組織承載，並以「提議」的方式交給各機構；城市則提供正當性、一張參與餐廳地圖，以及烹飪工作坊。這正是「可長可久的社會創新」的註冊商標：政府不強加行為，而是*搭建鷹架*，讓新規範能在生態系中橫向擴散——從餐廳傳到餐廳、從家長傳到家長。結果是韌性。由上而下強推的政策，會隨著通過它的那屆政府一起消亡；而透過公民社會長出來的規範，能跨越選舉週期而存活。這正是為什麼根特的星期四習慣，十七年後依然健在、也依然可被輸出。  
**對證據的清醒解讀。**誠實要求我們對數字保持謹慎。廣為流傳的說法——「半座城市都參與」、很高的彈性素食比例——多半來自熱情的單一來源，應視為「方向性參考」，而非經過查核的數據。更站得住腳的是它的*機制*：一個低成本、可退出（opt-out）、以夥伴關係為基礎的「推力（nudge）」，讓一種行為被正常化，並依城市自身的論述，支持其 2050 年達成氣候中和的目標。更深的結構侷限在於：自願性的「一日推力」，單憑自己無法撼動一整個「經濟上仍偏袒廉價肉品」的食物系統。蔬食日是一條文化的「入口匝道」，而非終點。它真正的價值，或許不在省下多少噸碳，而在於它安靜地證明了一件事：一座主流城市，可以在不靠強制的情況下，重新協商一條飲食規範。  
**可行的啟示與行動建議。**對*地方政府與各類機構*（醫院、大學、大型雇主）：可移植的動作不是「推廣素食」，而是「在一個食堂、一天之內，改掉一個預設值」，衡量取用率與餐盤剩食，讓成果自己說話。對*公民團體*：根特證明了「當提議者」比「當抗議者」更有力——帶給機構一份現成、低風險的套件（食譜、供應商清單、標示牌），讓它更容易點頭。對*餐廳與食品品牌*：設一個「蔬食日」是行銷資產與廚房研發的題目，而非犧牲。對*個人與家庭*：這堂課能縮小到一個家庭——你不必變成素食者才能改變你的足跡；你只需要改掉「一個預設值、一天」，剩下的交給新常態。根特最能輸出的產品，從來不是一道食譜，而是一套方法：移動預設值、保留自由、讓規範自己旅行。

**熱點三／Topic 3：紐澤西「倉庫屋頂社區太陽能」**

New Jersey's Community Solar on Warehouse Rooftops（住／社區能源・能源平權）

**來源連結 / Sources：** [Good Good Good（本週好消息）](https://goodgoodgood.co/articles/good-news-this-week-november-7-2026)； [Real Estate NJ（RE-NJ）](https://re-nj.com/bpu-approves-new-phase-of-community-solar-program-benefiting-warehouse-owners-residents/)； [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/solar-and-storage-boost-to-tackle-energy-affordability-in-new-jersey/)

### **【English Draft】Solar Power Without a Roof of Your Own: New Jersey's Warehouse-Top Community Solar as Social Innovation**

**Introduction.** This week's sustainability roundups spotlighted a quietly radical idea spreading across New Jersey: covering the vast, idle roofs of warehouses and logistics centres with solar panels, and then sending the savings not to the building's owner but to ordinary households nearby — many of them renters who will never own a panel. More than 51,000 households are reported to have subscribed, with the state now expanding its Community Solar programme toward some 3,000 MW of capacity. Strip away the megawatts and what you find is a genuine social innovation: a new financial and social architecture that detaches the *benefits* of solar from the *ownership* of a rooftop.  
**The problem it solves — and who was locked out.** Rooftop solar has long had an equity flaw hiding in plain sight. To benefit, you historically needed three things: a roof you own, good credit, and spare capital. That silently excluded the majority of people who most need lower energy bills — renters, apartment dwellers, lower-income families, anyone in shaded or north-facing homes. The result was a clean-energy transition that disproportionately subsidised wealthier homeowners. Community solar is the social innovation that unlocks this cage. Subscribers own no panels and sign no construction contract; they simply subscribe to a share of a solar array built elsewhere — on a warehouse roof — and receive a credit on their utility bill, typically a 15–25% discount. The roof and the household are financially linked without being physically connected.  
**Why the "warehouse" detail is the clever part.** The siting choice is where the model turns elegant. New Jersey is carpeted with enormous distribution warehouses — acres of flat, structurally sound, sun-exposed, otherwise-dead roof space. Putting solar there consumes no farmland, no forest, no new greenfield; it monetises an asset that was generating nothing. The warehouse owner gains lease income and a sustainability credential; the developer gains cheap, permit-friendly real estate; the subscriber gains a lower bill; the grid gains distributed local generation close to where power is used. This is the structural signature of strong social innovation — not a zero-sum transfer but a design in which several parties each gain from a previously wasted resource. It converts a symbol of overconsumption (the sprawling e-commerce warehouse) into an engine of distributed, equitable energy.  
**A balanced reading.** The model is not a panacea, and the reporting carries caveats worth respecting. Savings percentages vary by source and are sometimes vendor estimates; some projects have been slow to publish completion dates, pricing, or enrolment terms. Community solar still depends heavily on state policy scaffolding — mandated utility bill-credit mechanisms and programme caps — so it is only as durable as the political will behind it. And a discount on a bill, while real, is a modest lever against deep energy poverty; it eases the symptom of high costs without restructuring who owns the energy system. The most ambitious version of this innovation would push further — toward community *ownership* stakes, not just subscriptions, so that residents accrue equity and a voice, not merely a rebate.  
**Actionable insights.** For *policymakers elsewhere* (including dense Asian cities): the exportable lesson is to legislate the *bill-credit mechanism* first — the invisible plumbing that lets a kilowatt generated on a warehouse roof show up as a credit on a renter's bill — because without it, no amount of panels helps the people locked out. For *commercial property owners and logistics firms*: idle industrial roofs are a latent public asset; leasing them for community solar is low-risk income and a credible, non-greenwash sustainability story. For *community organisers and social enterprises*: the frontier is the *subscriber-ownership* model — cooperatives that let neighbours hold a stake, not just a discount. For *households*, especially renters: the quiet revelation is that you no longer need to own a roof, or anything at all, to participate in and benefit from the energy transition; you only need to subscribe. New Jersey's warehouse roofs make the point with concrete and silicon: the barrier to clean energy was never only technological — it was a question of who gets to benefit, and that is a design choice we can change.

### **【繁體中文初稿】不必擁有屋頂，也能用太陽能：紐澤西「倉庫屋頂社區太陽能」的社會創新**

**引言。**本週的永續新聞榜單，聚焦了一個正在紐澤西悄悄擴散、卻相當激進的點子：把倉庫與物流中心那片片廣闊而閒置的屋頂，鋪上太陽能板，然後把省下的電費，不是送給建物業主，而是送給附近的一般家庭——其中許多是永遠不會擁有一片太陽能板的「租屋族」。據報導已有超過 51,000 戶家庭訂閱，該州的「社區太陽能（Community Solar）」計畫，正朝約 3,000 MW 的裝置容量擴張。撥開這些百萬瓦的數字，你會看見一個真正的社會創新：一套全新的財務與社會架構，把太陽能的「好處」從屋頂的「所有權」中拆解開來。  
**它解決的問題——以及誰原本被鎖在門外。**屋頂太陽能長久以來，藏著一個明擺著卻沒人說的「公平性缺陷」。想享受好處，你歷來需要三樣東西：一個你自己擁有的屋頂、良好的信用，以及一筆閒錢。這悄悄地把「最需要降低電費」的大多數人排除在外——租屋族、公寓住戶、中低收入家庭，以及任何住在被遮蔽或朝北房子的人。結果是一場「反而更補貼有錢屋主」的能源轉型。社區太陽能，正是打開這個牢籠的社會創新。訂閱者不擁有任何面板、不簽任何工程合約；他們只是「訂閱」一座蓋在別處（倉庫屋頂）的太陽能電廠的一份額度，然後在自家電費單上獲得抵扣——通常是 15% 到 25% 的折扣。屋頂與家庭在財務上被連結，卻無須在物理上相連。  
**為什麼「倉庫」這個細節才是最聰明的地方。**選址，正是這個模式變得優雅之處。紐澤西遍地都是巨型的配送倉庫——一英畝接一英畝平坦、結構堅固、日照充足、原本完全「死掉」的屋頂空間。把太陽能放上去，不佔用任何農地、森林或新的綠地；它把一項「原本什麼都沒產出」的資產，變現了。倉庫業主獲得租金收入與永續招牌；開發商取得便宜又易於取得許可的「不動產」；訂閱者拿到更低的帳單；電網則獲得「靠近用電端」的分散式在地發電。這正是「強社會創新」的結構印記——不是零和的轉移，而是一種讓多方都能從「過去被浪費的資源」中各取所得的設計。它把「過度消費的象徵」（那些蔓延的電商倉庫），轉化成「分散而公平的能源引擎」。  
**平衡的解讀。**這個模式並非萬靈丹，相關報導也帶著值得尊重的但書。折扣比例因來源而異，有時只是廠商的估計值；部分案場遲遲未公布完工日期、價格或訂閱條件。社區太陽能仍高度仰賴州政府的政策鷹架——法定的電費抵扣機制與計畫上限——因此它有多耐久，就取決於背後的政治意志有多堅定。而帳單上的一筆折扣，雖然真實，面對深層的「能源貧窮」卻只是一根溫和的槓桿；它舒緩了高成本的症狀，卻沒有重構「誰擁有能源系統」。這個創新最具野心的版本，會再往前推一步——走向社區「持股（ownership）」，而不只是「訂閱」，讓居民累積的是股權與話語權，而非僅僅一筆退款。  
**可行的啟示與行動建議。**對*其他地方的決策者*（包括高密度的亞洲城市）：可輸出的一課，是先立法建立「電費抵扣機制」——那套讓「倉庫屋頂上發的一度電」能化為「租屋族帳單上一筆抵扣」的隱形管線；因為少了它，再多的面板也幫不了那些被鎖在門外的人。對*商用不動產業主與物流業者*：閒置的工業屋頂是一項潛在的公共資產；出租它來做社區太陽能，是低風險的收入，也是一個可信、而非漂綠的永續故事。對*社區組織者與社會企業*：前沿在於「訂閱者持股」模式——讓街坊鄰里持有的是股份，而不只是折扣的合作社。對*家庭*，尤其是租屋族：安靜的啟示是——你不再需要擁有一個屋頂、甚至不需要擁有任何東西，就能參與並受惠於能源轉型；你只需要「訂閱」。紐澤西的倉庫屋頂，用混凝土與矽，說明了一件事：通往乾淨能源的障礙，從來不只是技術——它是一個「誰能受惠」的問題，而這，是一個我們可以改變的設計選擇。
